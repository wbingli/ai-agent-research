# Tech Digest — 2026-11-03

**1. Git infrastructure meets agent-scale development**
OpenAI’s Pando backend supports parallel branch updates while preserving Git workflows; the company reports nearly 5× lower median push latency. The architectural tradeoff: readers inspecting multiple branches can see them at different points in time. [Read](https://openai.com/index/scaling-git-at-openai/)

**2. Why PostHog is moving off DuckDB**
PostHog is rebuilding its warehouse around Trino and HogLake after hitting single-node sizing, catalog, and Postgres-protocol compatibility limits. My takeaway: test unpredictable customer workloads and metadata growth—not just query benchmarks—before committing to a warehouse engine. [Read](https://posthog.com/blog/moving-off-duckdb)

**3. Figma’s case for specialized AI models**
Figma combines a post-trained editing model with frontier models, evaluating edits through structural checks, visual judges, and expert designers. The practical pattern is task-specific routing and evaluation; its newest benchmarked editing model remains unreleased, distinct from the model already in production. [Read](https://www.figma.com/blog/training-a-figma-fluent-editing-model/)

**4. Postgres previews built-in bloat cleanup**
Postgres 19’s release candidate includes concurrent REPACK, rebuilding tables while keeping reads and writes available for most of the operation. Before adopting it, budget for a second table/index copy and review the final exclusive lock and long-running-snapshot caveat—it isn’t maintenance without tradeoffs. [Read](https://www.snowflake.com/en/blog/engineering/postgres-19-repack-table-bloat/)

**5. A public exploit challenge tests AI-security hype**
A PHP2Shell challenge produced a confirmed, configuration-specific compromise, but only one participant successfully extracted the secret. The lesson: take the patching priority seriously without assuming AI makes every difficult exploit turnkey—or that low success rates imply safety. [Read](https://pavursec.com/blog/stealmybitcoin/)
