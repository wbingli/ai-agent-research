# Tech Digest — 2026-08-03

**1. Smaller, Faster, Safer: Running Kimi and GLM at Scale**
Cloudflare details how FP8 KV caches, INT4 weights, and shared-cache integrity checks improve large-model serving: Kimi concurrency doubled and peak throughput rose 41%, while GLM weights shrank about 40%. The practical lesson is to optimize prefill and decode separately—and treat denser multi-tenant inference as a security problem too. [Read](https://blog.cloudflare.com/smaller-faster-safer-models/)

**2. Massively Parallel Postgres Backups**
PlanetScale explains how it coordinates backup-only nodes, object storage, and WAL replay across sharded Postgres, reaching over 50 GB/s so petabyte-scale snapshots finish in hours without burdening production queries. It is a useful blueprint for designing backup and restore as distributed systems rather than maintenance scripts. [Read](https://planetscale.com/blog/massively-parallel-postgres-backups)

**3. SQLite Critical CVEs—or LLM Slop?**
JFrog investigated a batch of critical SQLite advisories and found fabricated functions, impossible bug mechanics, nonexistent patches, and proofs of concept that did not crash. Security teams should treat machine-generated vulnerability reports as untrusted inputs and require source-level reproduction before escalating or patching. [Read](https://research.jfrog.com/post/sqlite-critical-cves-or-llm-slops/)

**4. Frame Selection Is the Whole Game for Video LLMs**
A local video-understanding pipeline shows why uniform sampling wastes context on duplicates while missing brief but important events. Adaptive scene thresholds, color-aware deduplication, and transcript/frame synchronization turn a roughly 100–150-frame budget into a practical architecture for long-video analysis. [Read](https://leoaido.com/how-llms-watch-video/)

**5. AI Claims Ten Advances in Mathematics and Theoretical Computer Science**
OpenAI says its forthcoming Astra model resolved or materially advanced ten long-standing problems, with humans preparing manuscripts and Lean certificates used to formalize the arguments. If independent review holds up, this signals a shift from benchmark-solving toward AI-generated, machine-checkable research workflows. [Read](https://openai.com/index/ten-advances-in-mathematics/)
