# Tech Digest — 2026-10-27

**1. AI bug hunters complement—not replace—fuzzing**
Google’s six-month V8 comparison found 45 vulnerabilities with BigSleep versus 37 with Fuzzilli, with each finding bugs the other missed. The practical takeaway: add agentic security analysis alongside fuzzing rather than replacing a proven testing layer. [Read](https://projectzero.google/2026/10/fuzzing-v-AI.html)

**2. What keeps 1,000 agent-written PRs from becoming chaos?**
Kiro Crew combines specialized reviewers, real-app tests, and a readiness gate requiring every expected check to run and pass; only simple-tier PRs auto-merge. Its most reusable lesson: stop review/fix loops from endlessly expanding a small change into an unnecessary subsystem. [Read](https://kiro.dev/blog/software-factory-stability/)

**3. Search engines borrow a database performance playbook**
Turbopuffer’s planned v3 processes queries through an end-to-end vectorized pipeline, batching work to reduce per-row dispatch overhead and improve CPU utilization. Worth studying for retrieval services moving beyond simple top-k searches toward richer filtering and aggregation—not a production release yet. [Read](https://turbopuffer.com/blog/fully-vectorized-search-engine)

**4. Tailscale SSH needs an access-policy review**
Tailscale is investigating privilege-escalation issues involving already-authorized SSH users, with fixes still undergoing testing in its published update. Review access to sensitive hosts and disable unnecessary SSH forwarding; the company is not recommending a blanket shutdown of Tailscale SSH. [Read](https://tailscale.com/ssh-investigation-update)

**5. Small multimodal models target specialized, local AI**
Liquid AI’s open-weight LFM3 family starts around 2B parameters, spanning text, vision, audio, and decision models on a shared hybrid backbone. For bounded workloads, it offers another candidate to evaluate for local fine-tuning and inference rather than defaulting to a large hosted model. [Read](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm3)
