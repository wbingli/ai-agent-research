# Tech Digest — 2026-09-30

**1. Gemini 4 Argon targets longer-running engineering work**
Google announced a model built for extended coding and enterprise workflows, with an output limit of one million tokens. Access starts with trusted cyber defenders—not a general developer rollout yet—so treat this as a signal for future agent evaluations, not an immediate migration. [Read](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-argon/)

**2. Netlify cuts edge latency by bringing compute in-house**
Netlify reports median warm invocations of 5–6ms, down from 25–40ms, after replacing an external execution service with Firecracker microVMs inside its edge network. The useful architecture lesson: remove network detours, preserve warm-instance affinity, and relax that affinity when traffic creates hotspots. [Read](https://www.netlify.com/blog/edge-functions-firecracker-microvms/)

**3. An unchecked JWT signature exposed an analytics service**
A researcher reports that Microsoft’s Titan service checked token claims but skipped signature verification, enabling unauthorized SQL queries; the endpoint was subsequently locked down. The headline’s 17.3 trillion rows describe estimated reachable storage—not a confirmed mass breach—a reminder to test authentication failures, not just successful logins. [Read](https://blog.faav.net/how-i-couldve-accessed-17-trillion-microsoft-records)

**4. When a database-backed queue needs Kafka**
Tigris moved asynchronous cleanup work to Kafka after queue scans and repeated writes began competing with customer traffic in FoundationDB, while retaining database queues elsewhere. The key tradeoff: losing atomicity across systems was acceptable for delayed cleanup, not something to apply indiscriminately to every workload. [Read](https://www.tigrisdata.com/blog/quick-fdb-kafka/)

**5. TLA+ cannot certify an AI-written system by itself**
Hillel Wayne explains why formal verification only helps with properties you can actually specify, and why a correct design does not automatically mean correct implementation. For agent-assisted engineering, use model checking for explicit concurrency guarantees rather than treating it as a blanket correctness stamp. [Read](https://buttondown.com/hillelwayne/archive/what-tla-can-and-cant-check/)
