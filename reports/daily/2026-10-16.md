# Tech Digest — 2026-10-16

**1. Large-model inference across desktop Macs**
Apple reports over 36 tokens/sec for 4-bit Muse Spark Open across four M5 Ultra Mac Studios, using MLX and Thunderbolt RDMA. For private AI deployments, the useful lesson is the tradeoff: pipeline parallelism expands capacity, while tensor parallelism needs low-latency links to accelerate generation. [Read](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/mlx-distributed-inference)

**2. Redis adds Roaring compressed bitmaps**
Merged support, expected in Redis 8.12, lets sparse integer sets avoid the memory and scanning costs of ordinary bitmaps. Worth benchmarking for audience cohorts and targeting intersections—but the measured benefits depend on data distribution, not compression alone. [Read](https://lemire.me/blog/2026/10/15/redis-now-has-compressed-bitmaps-roaring/)

**3. AVIF still wins many web-image workloads**
A detailed comparison finds AVIF generally smaller and faster to decode than JPEG XL across the tested web images, with exceptions. For artwork and thumbnail pipelines, benchmark representative assets and encoder settings rather than assuming a newer format cuts bandwidth. [Read](https://jakearchibald.com/2026/jpeg-xl-vs-avif/)

**4. A decades-old counter bug reaches kernel root**
Researchers chained a signed-counter overflow with another kernel flaw to gain local root on OpenBSD despite multiple mitigations; both issues have fixes. The defensive lesson: audit legacy counters and allocator predictability, not just whether hardening features are enabled. [Read](https://calif.io/research/big-fish)

**5. Claude reconstructs NIST curve-seed origins**
Anthropic reports that an internal research model traced five NIST prime-curve seeds to a timestamp-seeded C random-number generator, with reproducible verification code. This resolves part of a cryptographic provenance mystery—not an ECDSA break or a reason to rotate keys—and some selection details remain unexplained. [Read](https://www.anthropic.com/research/ecdsa-curve-origins)
