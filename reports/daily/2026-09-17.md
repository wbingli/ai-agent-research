# Tech Digest — 2026-09-17

**1. Stop retry storms by tracking who owns the error**
Uber’s service mesh distinguishes errors originating in a service from failures merely propagated upstream, suppressing redundant retries along dependency chains. It’s a concrete pattern for preventing one unhealthy backend from overwhelming an entire API stack. [Read](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/protecting-against-retry-storms/)

**2. Rust maintainers targeted through fake video calls**
Rust warns of an active campaign using plausible job and project outreach to trick maintainers into installing software or running commands, aiming to compromise package publishing. Brief maintainers on the tactic, use trusted meeting platforms, and review account access and MFA. [Read](https://blog.rust-lang.org/2026/09/17/targeted-attacks/)

**3. A 27B model compressed into 5.9GB**
PrismML’s Apache-licensed Bonsai 2 uses ternary weights and reportedly retains 98.2% of its full-precision parent’s aggregate benchmark performance. Worth testing for private, local agent workloads—but benchmark retention isn’t a guarantee of reliability on your own tasks. [Read](https://prismml.com/news/bonsai-2-27b)

**4. Full-text search built around Postgres internals**
PlanetScale’s new TIN index uses native tuple identifiers and vectorized bitmap operations to support ranked search, concurrent updates, and transactional visibility. The architecture is worth studying if you want search inside Postgres rather than another synchronized datastore; validate its vendor benchmarks against your workload. [Read](https://planetscale.com/blog/introducing-tin)

**5. When redundant clocks share the same wrong answer**
A new analysis of Telstra’s July outage explains how stale GPS firmware and circular timing dependencies spread a 2006 date across its network. The lesson: audit time-source independence, version-control timing configurations, and monitor clock infrastructure as a critical service. [Read](https://www.netnod.se/blog/telstra-outage-night-network-decided-year-was-2006)
