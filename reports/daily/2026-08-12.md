# Tech Digest — 2026-08-12

**1. Tailscale finds a 16-year-old SQLite WAL bug**
Months of control-plane outages led Tailscale and SQLite engineers to a rare WAL-reset race that could corrupt databases despite a conventional single-writer design. The forensic timeline is a strong playbook for debugging failures in “boring,” trusted infrastructure. [Read](https://tailscale.com/blog/sqlite-wal-reset-bug)

**2. Qwen releases a 2.4T-parameter open-weight model**
Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B brings the vendor’s Max-class model to an open release, targeting coding, research, professional work, and long-horizon agents. It expands the options for teams evaluating frontier-scale models without committing entirely to closed APIs. [Read](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B)

**3. Delta rethinks collaboration around coding agents**
Zed’s Delta keeps code, agent conversations, and review comments synchronized through a replicated worktree while preserving normal Git workflows. The design points toward reviews centered on evolving intent and context rather than static commit snapshots. [Read](https://zed.dev/blog/introducing-delta)

**4. A Forgejo compromise offers a practical patching postmortem**
An internet-exposed Forgejo instance running an end-of-life release was compromised through a recently patched remote-code-execution flaw. The incident highlights the operational risk of pinned container tags without automated lifecycle alerts, plus the value of resource monitoring as an early intrusion signal. [Read](https://phunky.cafe/my-homelab-got-hacked/)

**5. Mojo reaches 1.0 with a stability commitment**
Mojo 1.0 marks the language as production-ready and shifts its 1.x evolution toward primarily additive changes. That stability promise matters for teams considering Mojo for performance-sensitive AI infrastructure but previously deterred by frequent breaking changes. [Read](https://www.modular.com/blog/modular-26-5-mojo-1-0-is-here)
